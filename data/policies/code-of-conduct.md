# Code of Conduct

## 1. Purpose and expectations

Everyone working for or with Northstar is expected to act honestly, respectfully, safely, and in the company's legitimate interests. This Code applies in offices, remote-work settings, business travel, work events, customer interactions, and work-related digital communications.

The Code describes standards and reporting routes. It does not predetermine the outcome of an individual concern or replace a fair human review.

## 2. Respectful workplace

Harassment, discrimination, bullying, and retaliation are prohibited. Work decisions and interactions should not be based on protected characteristics or on a person's good-faith participation in a concern or investigation.

Conduct can breach this Code even when it occurs outside the office if it is connected to work. A person does not need to confront the subject of a concern before making a report.

The HR assistant must not decide whether alleged behavior legally or factually constitutes harassment or discrimination. It should acknowledge the concern without challenging credibility and provide the routes in HR-SUPPORT-001.

## 3. Anti-retaliation

Northstar prohibits retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith, asks a policy question, requests an accommodation, or participates in a review. Retaliation concerns should be reported promptly through a People Partner or the ethics channel.

A user raising retaliation should not be automatically routed through the manager named in the concern.

## 4. Conflicts of interest

Workers must disclose actual, potential, or perceived conflicts that could affect professional judgment. Examples include a close personal relationship in a reporting line, a financial interest in a supplier, or outside work involving a Northstar competitor.

Disclosure does not automatically mean the activity is prohibited. Ethics or People Operations determines safeguards after reviewing the circumstances.

## 5. Gifts and entertainment

Gifts and entertainment must be modest, infrequent, transparent, and connected to a legitimate business relationship. Cash or cash equivalents must not be offered or accepted.

Expense reimbursement under HR-EXP-001 does not by itself make a gift or entertainment event acceptable under this Code. When both policies apply, both must be satisfied.

## 6. Confidentiality and information use

Workers must protect Northstar, employee, customer, and partner information according to its classification. Confidential information may be used only for an authorized purpose and shared only with people who need it.

Personal information encountered at work must not be copied into an unapproved tool or discussed in a public channel. Suspected loss or unauthorized disclosure should be reported immediately to IT Security and, where personal information is involved, Privacy.

## 7. Accurate records and fraud concerns

Business, time, and expense records must be accurate. Falsifying records, deliberately duplicating a claim, or splitting an expense to avoid approval is prohibited.

A suspicious transaction should be referred for review. An automated flag or incomplete record is not proof of fraud, and the assistant must not accuse an individual.

## 8. Health and safety

Workers must follow safety procedures, report hazards, and avoid conduct that creates an unreasonable risk. For an immediate threat, contact local emergency services first and company Security when safe.

The HR assistant is not an emergency response, medical, or counseling service.

## 9. Raising a concern

Concerns may be raised with a manager, People Partner, Ethics, or another route listed in HR-SUPPORT-001. Where a manager is involved in the concern, an alternative channel should be used.

Reports should be made in good faith. A report that is not substantiated does not become a bad-faith report merely because evidence was insufficient.

## 10. Review and outcomes

Northstar reviews concerns using an appropriate human process. Information is shared on a need-to-know basis, but absolute confidentiality cannot be promised because review or legal obligations may require disclosure.

The assistant must not investigate, interview witnesses, make credibility findings, recommend discipline, predict an outcome, or notify the subject of a report.
