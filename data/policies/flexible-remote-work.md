# Flexible and Remote Work Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy governs recurring and ad hoc work away from an employee's assigned office while the employee remains in their normal country of work. Working from another country is governed by HR-WORK-002.

Flexible working supports productivity and inclusion while preserving customer service, security, and team collaboration. The maximum frequencies in this policy are not guaranteed entitlements.

## 2. Eligibility and work arrangement

An office-based hybrid employee may request up to two remote-working days per working week. An employee still in probation may request up to one remote day per week.

An employee whose profile identifies the role as fully remote follows that recorded arrangement rather than the hybrid default. On-site operational duties may be ineligible, but eligibility must come from the employee record; it must not be inferred from job title alone.

Permanent and fixed-term employees may request an arrangement. Intern schedules are determined by the placement program and manager.

## 3. Approval

A recurring schedule requires manager approval and should identify the normal remote days, review date, and arrangements for office events. An ad hoc remote day follows the team's established approval process.

A manager may approve less than the stated maximum based on operational needs. More than two days per week for a hybrid employee—or more than one during probation—requires People Operations review and a documented exception.

An approved arrangement may be reviewed when the employee changes role, team, work location, or performance support needs. Changes should be discussed rather than applied retrospectively.

## 4. Availability and working time

Employees should be reachable during their agreed hours and normally available during core collaboration hours of 10:00–16:00 in their assigned office's local time. Flexible start and finish times outside the core hours require agreement with the manager.

Remote work does not change the employee's standard hours, overtime rules, absence-reporting duties, or assigned office location.

## 5. Workspace, equipment, and costs

Employees are responsible for maintaining a reasonably safe and suitable workspace. Northstar provides a standard laptop and approved accessories. Additional home-office equipment is governed by HR-EXP-001 and the approved-equipment list.

Normal household costs, including rent, utilities, and general internet service, are not reimbursable unless an approved accommodation or local requirement says otherwise.

## 6. Security and confidentiality

Employees must use a company-managed device, approved network controls, and required multifactor authentication. Confidential work must not be performed where conversations or screens can be observed by unauthorized people.

Company work must not be conducted on an unauthorized shared device. Suspected loss, unauthorized access, or data exposure must be reported immediately to IT Security.

## 7. Attendance at the office

Employees may be asked to attend the assigned office for customer work, training, planning, or team events with reasonable notice. A normal remote day does not automatically override an essential in-person requirement.

Travel from home to the assigned office is an ordinary commute and is not normally reimbursable.

## 8. Accommodations and sensitive requests

A request related to disability, health, caregiving, or another potentially protected circumstance must be routed to the confidential People Operations process. The assistant and manager may explain the process but must not assess medical need or demand sensitive details.

## 9. Exceptions and escalation

Escalate to People Operations when:

- The requested frequency exceeds the standard limit.
- Eligibility is not recorded or is disputed.
- The request concerns an accommodation or sensitive personal circumstances.
- The employee wants to change their assigned country or office.
- Security, working-time, or cross-border questions arise.
