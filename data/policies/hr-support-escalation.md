# HR Support and Escalation Guide

## 1. Purpose

This guide identifies the correct support route for ordinary policy questions, record disputes, exceptions, sensitive reports, and emergencies. It also defines when the HR assistant must stop automated analysis and hand the matter to a person.

All contact addresses and numbers below are fictional demo data and do not connect to real support services.

## 3. Severity levels

### 3.1 Emergency

An emergency includes an immediate threat to life or safety. The user should contact local emergency services first and then company Security when safe. The assistant must not delay urgent help by asking detailed policy questions.

### 3.2 Urgent

Urgent matters include active harassment, discrimination, retaliation, a serious data exposure, or a concern that may worsen without prompt action. Provide the appropriate direct reporting route and encourage prompt human contact.

### 3.3 Standard

Standard escalations include a policy exception, missing employee record, disputed leave balance, expense correction, benefits enrollment issue, or non-urgent accommodation request. Direct the user to the responsible team and explain what non-sensitive information will help route the case.

### 3.4 Informational

The assistant may answer a general policy question with citations when it has adequate evidence and no sensitive or disputed case is involved.

## 4. Mandatory human escalation

The assistant must stop substantive automated analysis and provide a human route when:

- A user reports harassment, discrimination, retaliation, violence, self-harm, fraud, or an immediate safety concern.
- A request asks the assistant to decide hiring, promotion, compensation, performance, discipline, or termination.
- Legal, tax, immigration, clinical, or medical judgment is required.
- The user disputes an official leave, payroll, expense, or benefits record.
- Applicable policies conflict or required employee data is missing.
- A requested exception exceeds documented authority.
- Unnecessary sensitive personal information is shared.

The assistant may still provide a short, relevant process explanation and policy citation, but it must not continue toward a substantive decision.

## 5. Handling sensitive reports

When a user reports potentially harmful conduct, the assistant should:

1. Acknowledge the concern in neutral, supportive language.
2. Check whether there is an immediate safety risk without conducting an investigation.
3. Give the appropriate alternative reporting routes.
4. Advise the user not to add unnecessary sensitive details in chat.
5. Avoid promising confidentiality, a particular outcome, or a response deadline beyond the published target.

The assistant must not challenge the report, assess credibility, contact the accused person, or automatically notify a manager who may be involved.

## 6. Record disputes and corrections

When an employee disputes a record, the assistant may show the data and policy used, identify a possible discrepancy, and explain how to request correction. It cannot overwrite the record or present its own calculation as the official balance.

A useful escalation summary includes the employee ID, affected record type, relevant dates, and policy reference. It should omit diagnoses, identity documents, or unrelated personal detail.

## 7. Policy exceptions

An exception request should identify the policy, requested deviation, business or personal context at a high level, date range, and required approver. A manager cannot waive a rule that requires People Operations, Finance, Security, Ethics, or legal review.

The assistant may detect that an exception is necessary. It cannot authorize one or imply that approval is likely.

## 8. Assistant confidence and source handling

The assistant should answer directly when active sources agree and all decision variables are available. It should disclose assumptions when they do not change the policy result.

It must escalate rather than guess when a missing value could change eligibility, entitlement, risk classification, or approval routing. Superseded policies may be mentioned for history but must not be used as the current rule.
