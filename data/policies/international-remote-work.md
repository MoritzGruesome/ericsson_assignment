# International Remote Work Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy governs employee-requested temporary work from a country other than the employee's assigned work location. It is intended for short, occasional arrangements and does not create a right to relocate.

International remote work may involve immigration, tax, employment, information-security, insurance, and customer-contract risks. Approval is never guaranteed, even when a request is within the numeric limits below.

## 2. Eligibility

An employee must be permanent and must have completed probation before the proposed start date. A fixed-term employee may request an exception but is not eligible through the standard process.

The employee must have satisfactory connectivity and a role that can be performed remotely without breaching security, customer, regulatory, or time-zone requirements. Role eligibility must come from an employee or role record, not an assumption based on title.

## 3. Duration limits

An eligible employee may be approved for up to 20 international remote-working days in one policy year. A single trip may include no more than 10 working days of international remote work.

Annual-leave days are not counted toward the 20-day international remote-work limit when the dates are clearly recorded as leave. Weekends and local non-working days are also excluded unless the employee actually works on those days under an approved schedule.

Employer-directed business travel is governed by travel procedures and does not consume this allowance.

## 4. Advance notice and approval

The employee must normally submit a request at least 30 calendar days before the proposed first working day abroad. The request must include:

- Destination country and city.
- Arrival and departure dates.
- Exact working days and annual-leave days.
- Working hours and time-zone plan.
- A description of data and systems accessed.
- Confirmation that the employee will comply with entry and work-authorization requirements.

Manager and People Operations approval are required. IT Security review is also required for roles that access restricted data or when the destination record calls for it.

## 5. Destination assessment

The destination must appear on Northstar's approved-destination dataset on the date the request is assessed. Destination records are effective-dated and may contain conditions or require additional review.

An approved-destination entry means only that Northstar permits a request to proceed through review. It is not confirmation of an employee's right to work, immigration status, tax treatment, or personal safety.

Restricted or missing destinations must be referred to People Operations and Security. The assistant must not recommend bypassing a restriction by recording the days as annual leave while work is performed.

## 6. Immigration, tax, and legal responsibility

Employees must provide accurate travel and work information and are responsible for holding appropriate permission to enter and remain in the destination. Northstar assesses company risk before approval.

The HR assistant may identify that a review is required but must never state that a visa permits work, calculate tax residency, or provide legal or immigration advice.

## 7. Security and customer obligations

Employees must use company-managed equipment and approved security controls. Work involving export-controlled, regulated, customer-restricted, or highly sensitive data may be prohibited from some destinations.

IT Security approval is mandatory when the employee profile indicates restricted-data access. A manager cannot waive this review.

## 8. Expenses, insurance, and working conditions

International remote work is employee-requested. Travel, accommodation, visas, insurance, connectivity, and other personal costs are not reimbursable unless separately authorized for a business purpose.

Employees remain subject to their normal working hours and time-recording responsibilities. They must agree a schedule that supports their team and does not create unsafe working hours.

## 9. Combining remote work with leave

An employee may combine annual leave and international remote work when each date is clearly categorized. Example: five approved working days abroad followed by five annual-leave days consumes five international remote-working days and five annual-leave days.

Approval of annual leave does not imply approval to work internationally, and approval to work internationally does not approve adjacent leave.

## 10. Mandatory escalation

Compliance review and human approval are required for every international remote-work request. Escalate when:

- A trip exceeds 10 working days or the annual total would exceed 20.
- The employee is in probation or is not permanent.
- The destination is restricted, missing, or subject to changed conditions.
- The employee accesses restricted data.
- The request gives rise to immigration, tax, insurance, security, or customer concerns.
- Notice is shorter than 30 calendar days.
- The classification of business travel, leave, and remote work is unclear.
